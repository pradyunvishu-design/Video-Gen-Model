# Human Script Rubric

Score the approved spoken draft from 1-10. `clarity`, `natural_speech`, `promise_delivery`, and `voice_consistency` must reach 9. Every other dimension must reach 8. A major or blocking issue fails the script regardless of averages.

| Dimension | Passing evidence |
|---|---|
| Clarity | Terms arrive after the problem, pronouns have obvious subjects, and each sentence carries one followable idea. |
| Natural speech | The wording survives a read-aloud pass, contractions feel natural, and sentence rhythm varies without theatrical performance. |
| Useful density | Each chapter adds proof, a concrete example, a consequence, a limit, or a decision. |
| Continuity | Chapters feel like one argument from one speaker; bridges are causal or clean cuts. |
| Originality | No copied persona, creator catchphrase, template hook, or generic AI-summary language. |
| Visual proof | Important claims have something real the edit can show; visuals prove or clarify rather than decorate. |
| Teaching value | A viewer can explain the central mechanism and apply a practical rule afterward. |
| Promise delivery | The hook makes one truthful promise, proof arrives early, and the ending answers it. |
| Voice consistency | Audience relationship, skepticism, vocabulary, and humor restraint remain stable without repeated verbal tics. |

The opening also fails when it is unsupported, interchangeable with another topic, built from a stock viral-hook shell, or unable to point to visible proof. Generate four evidence-backed candidates across at least three mechanisms before drafting the full script.

## Read-aloud pass

Listen for the following rather than merely checking grammar:

1. Where would a speaker run out of breath?
2. Where could the listener lose the subject of `it`, `this`, `they`, or `that`?
3. Does a transition sound like spoken logic or a generated signpost?
4. Does the joke come from the evidence, or was it inserted because the prompt requested humor?
5. Does every chapter alter what the listener understands?
6. Does the final judgment answer the opening promise without a generic recap?
7. Does the opening name a concrete change, result, friction, or comparison before asking for attention?
8. Do brief reactions follow evidence rather than substitute for it?
