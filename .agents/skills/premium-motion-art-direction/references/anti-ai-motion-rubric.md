# Anti-AI Motion Rubric

This is a rejection gate, not a request to add more decoration.

## Reject immediately

- Invented mascots, ears, faces, bodies, or personalities attached to a company logo.
- A centered logo object plus generic headline as the complete concept.
- Stacked all-caps headlines outside short labels or genuinely urgent news typography.
- Perfectly mirrored layouts, equal card grids, generic bento boards, and repeated pill badges.
- Plastic 3D icons, ambient orange/blue glow, glass surfaces, decorative particles, or gradients with no factual role.
- Motion that exists to prove animation happened: floating, bobbing, spinning, orbiting, constant zoom, or parallax drift.
- Fake UI, invented metrics, warped marks, generic “AI” labels, and synthetic documentary imagery.

## Require instead

- One source- or product-specific proof object that would not make sense in a different story.
- Intentional asymmetry with a clear first read, second read, and takeaway.
- Official marks unmodified and localized; company color belongs to the mark or one active state.
- Density variation across the episode: open reset, dense proof, sequential mechanism, and quiet conclusion.
- One restrained memorable move tied to meaning, with a locked camera and cut/fade edit grammar.
- Typography and whitespace doing most of the work; effects remain subordinate.

## Source text annotations

An underline passes only when the captured artifact contains:

1. the exact visible phrase,
2. a browser-measured single-line DOM Range,
3. normalized coordinates stored beside the artifact, and
4. narration that directly discusses that phrase.

If any item is missing, use no underline. A tight element outline is acceptable only when the element boundary itself is the narrated evidence.

## Five-dimension critique

Score each dimension from 0–5: philosophy, hierarchy, detail/execution, function/specificity, innovation/restraint. Any score below 3 fails. Philosophy below 4 requires a new concept rather than surface polish.
